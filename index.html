About

I build at the edge of data science, clinical informatics, and product strategy.

My path started in evolutionary ecology — I did my Ph.D. at Yale studying how intraspecific variation affects species coexistence. But my curiosity pulled me toward the computational side, and I found my footing in large-scale genomic data at Dana-Farber, where I now lead both technical strategy and product development for AI-enabled clinical research platforms.

What drives me is the belief that the hardest problems in healthcare are often bottlenecks in tooling and data access — not biology. I combine deep bioinformatics expertise (WGS, single-cell RNA-seq, variant calling, multi-omic integration across 50,000+ tumor samples) with product-minded thinking: understanding users, mapping friction points, and shipping systems that real clinicians and researchers actually use.

I don't believe in "build it and they will come." I'd rather spend time at the whiteboard with a research nurse figuring out what slows her down than build in a vacuum.
